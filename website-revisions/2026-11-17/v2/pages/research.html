Reliable learning with limited resources

I develop trustworthy and resource-efficient AI for decisions under limits on data, computation, energy, and latency. My research connects graph learning theory, adaptive LLM inference, compact neural architectures, and networked autonomy. A growing direction is AI service operations: deciding when a system should compute further, verify an answer, or escalate a decision.

1 · Statistical foundations and robustness of graph learning

I study what message-passing graph neural networks can learn, how much data they need, and how graph structure affects their limitations. My ICLR 2026 work establishes minimax sample-complexity lower bounds in specified inductive and transductive settings. Related research examines adversarial vulnerabilities in GNN-based wireless resource management.

Current direction: Structural poisoning and label transfer. A sole-author manuscript under review studies exact randomized minimax risk on a constructed bounded-degree graph family and distinguishes information loss from implementation-dependent failures to transfer observed labels.

2 · Efficient architectures and adaptive inference

I develop compact graph models, constrained low-rank training methods, and inference procedures that adapt computation to the task. Applications include radio resource management, flow-based UAV coverage, and efficient language-model reasoning.

Selected work: Tiny Graph Neural Networks (tinyML 2024); Tiny Flow Matching (IEEE MLSP 2026, to appear); CGES: Confidence-Guided Early Stopping (NeurIPS 2025 Efficient Reasoning Workshop, Spotlight). The extended CGES manuscript is under review at TMLR.

Current direction: Certified adaptive-depth graph controllers for power-capped AI cluster scheduling. A sole-author manuscript under review derives a stopping certificate for a specified class of contractive controllers and evaluates decision preservation and computational cost in trace-calibrated simulations.

3 · Generative methods for networked autonomy

I connect learned generative maps with trajectory design, coverage objectives, and resource allocation. OurNeurIPS 2026 paper develops ergodic trajectory design through learned pushforward maps and conditional flow matching. Related projects study robust non-uniform coverage, graph attention, and low-rank velocity models for UAVs.

Funded research: NSF CPS Small AERIAL (CNS-2528914), 2025–2028. As Co-PI, I lead work on budget-aware learning, robustness and security evaluation, and latency–energy–reliability analysis.

Scientific data and interdisciplinary collaboration

My interdisciplinary collaboration provides methodological guidance on generative modeling and evaluation of small health datasets, including maternal nutrition and human-milk composition. This work is distinct from my graduate and undergraduate research advising. Current undergraduate projects include drone digital twins, multi-UAV orchestration, and deploying flow-matching and Tiny Flow Matching methods on a robot; deployment work is in progress.
